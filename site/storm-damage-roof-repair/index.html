Detail
The detail

Storm damage roofing in Oklahoma, and what to actually do about it

Every neighbourhood in Oklahoma gets hit at some point. That is not a sales line, it is the reality of the market, and it is why storm work is a significant part of what any roofing company here does.

Wind damage versus hail damage

Wind lifts and tears. It removes shingles outright, creases them where they have folded back, and breaks the seal strips that hold each course down. Wind damage is generally the more visible of the two, and a shingle in your yard is the clearest possible sign of it.

Hail bruises. As described above, the impact fractures the shingle mat and dislodges granules. The shingle is still on the roof, still looks roughly normal from below, and is now degrading. This is the damage type that people miss.

The evidence your inspector should be collecting

Impact marks on the field, across every elevation rather than one convenient slope.
Granular loss, both on the roof and accumulating in the gutters below it.
Soft metals: vents, caps and flashing, which take a visible dent from hail that shingles absorb invisibly.
Window screens and fencing, which mark readily and corroborate the event.
Directionality, meaning which elevations were hit and which were not.

Why the adjuster meeting matters

An adjuster is assessing a lot of properties in a compressed period after a significant storm. Having a roofer present who has already been on the roof, has documented what is there, and can point to specific evidence changes the quality of that conversation.

Where our assessment and the adjuster's differ, there is usually some back and forth about scope. That is normal. We work through it with the carrier rather than leaving you to negotiate a technical roofing argument on your own.

Decking and supplementation

Decking condition cannot be assessed through an existing roof. When a replacement is approved and the tear off exposes decking that is damaged or does not meet current code, that gets documented for supplementation. It is a normal part of the process and it exists so the approved scope covers what the roof genuinely requires.

What we install afterwards

Where it makes sense given the hail exposure here, we try to get customers into a class 4 impact rated shingle, which is built to handle hail better than a standard product. It is worth asking your carrier how they treat impact rated roofing, because some treat it differently on premiums.

We will not install a shingle we are not prepared to warranty ourselves. Every roof we install carries a two year written workmanship warranty, and qualifying customers can purchase upgraded GAF, Owens Corning, CertainTeed or Malarkey manufacturer warranties in 25 and 30 year options.

Storm FAQs

Storm and hail damage questions

Straight answers, including the ones about what we cannot do.

Do you handle the insurance paperwork?

Yes. We take care of the roofing paperwork on the insurance side, meet your adjuster at the property, and work through the approved scope with your carrier where there is disagreement about what the damage actually is.

When a replacement is required, we document code related decking conditions for supplementation, so the approved scope reflects what the roof genuinely needs.

Can you guarantee my claim will be approved?

No, and you should be cautious of any roofer who says otherwise. Your carrier and their adjuster determine whether a claim is approved and what it covers. That decision is not ours to make.

What we can do is document the damage properly, be at the property when the adjuster is there, and make the case for the scope the roof actually needs. That is genuinely worth having, but it is not a guarantee.

How soon after a storm should I call?

Sooner is better, mainly because insurance policies have time limits on reporting damage and because a documented inspection close to the event is stronger evidence than one months later.

That said, we do not run a 24 hour emergency dispatch and we do not advertise one. Calls after 5pm go to voicemail. The contact form is open around the clock, and the office schedules an inspector out from there.

What does hail actually do to a roof?

Usually bruising rather than holes. The impact fractures the mat of the shingle and knocks the protective granules loose. From the ground this is invisible; from the roof it looks like scattered dark spots.

The consequence is that the asphalt underneath is now exposed to ultraviolet light at those points and begins degrading. The failure arrives later, which is why an inspection after a storm matters even when the roof looks fine.

My neighbours all got new roofs. Does that mean I need one?

Not necessarily. Hail is directional, and what matters is the condition of your roof, not what happened next door. The mistake to avoid is calling in a claim before a trustworthy professional has assessed the roof, even after a bad storm.

If only one slope took damage, your carrier may pay for repairs on that slope rather than a replacement. That can leave you under your deductible, paying out of pocket, with one slope that no longer matches the rest of the roof. We check every elevation first, then tell you honestly whether a claim is worth filing.

What about the gutters, screens and fencing?

They are part of the picture, and our inspection covers them. Soft metals, window screens and fencing mark more readily than shingles do, so they help establish what a storm actually did.

They are also frequently damaged in their own right. It is worth having them documented at the same time rather than discovering it after the claim has closed.
