Materials
Materials

What we install

Six specialty material families, each with different structural and detailing requirements.

Clay tile

The traditional Mediterranean and Spanish profile. Beautiful, heavy, brittle, and entirely dependent on what sits underneath it.

Concrete tile

Similar profiles and appearance to clay with different weight and cost characteristics. Common across the metro.

Composite and synthetic tile

Lighter and considerably less brittle than natural material, which matters on structures not designed for the load. DaVinci, Brava and Grand Manor among others.

Natural slate

The traditional material on older and higher end properties. Long lived and completely unforgiving of a rushed installation.

Synthetic slate

The appearance of slate with a fraction of the weight. Frequently the right answer on a structure that cannot carry natural stone.

Wood and synthetic shake

The traditional shake appearance, in natural cedar or in a synthetic product that removes the maintenance and fire considerations.

The
The method

What a correct tile installation actually involves

The visible part of a tile roof is the least important part of it. What decides whether it performs for decades is entirely underneath, and invisible the moment the last tile is set.

Underlayment, battens and layout

On a tile roof the underlayment does far more work than it does under asphalt. Tile sheds the bulk of the water, but wind driven rain gets underneath it routinely, and the underlayment is what actually keeps the building dry.

Fastening, flashing and how the crew moves

Tile has to be secured against Oklahoma wind loads without cracking the material or creating a path for water. Every hip, valley, wall junction and penetration is then flashed in metal and detailed by hand. On a complex tile roof there are a great many of these.

Repair, matching and product we trust

A lot of our specialty work is repair rather than installation, and the hard part is matching: finding product close enough to what is there that the repair does not read as a patch from the street. We maintain specialty roofs in Nichols Hills, Gaillardia, Rose Creek, Cobblestone and Heritage Hills.

Specialty FAQs

Tile and slate questions

The things people ask when they have a roof most contractors will not touch.

Why do so few roofers in Oklahoma City quote tile?

Because it is expensive and unforgiving. In the owner's words: not many companies want to do this work, because it costs a lot and a mistake is a big deal.

That is genuinely the situation. Tile and slate materials cost a great deal more than asphalt, the install is slower, and errors are expensive to correct. It is also why there is not much competition for this work in the metro, and why we do a lot of it.

What makes a correct tile installation different from an ordinary roof?

Almost everything that matters happens before the tile goes down. Underlayment, batten layout and fastening determine whether the roof performs, and the flashing at every hip, valley and penetration has to be detailed by hand rather than filled with sealant.

Tile is also heavy and brittle. Walking it incorrectly damages it, so how the crew moves on the roof during the install is part of the method rather than an afterthought.

What specialty materials do you install?

Clay, concrete, composite and synthetic tile. Natural slate and synthetic slate. Wood shake and synthetic shake.

We are a DaVinci Masterpiece contractor and we work with Brava and Grand Manor products among others. Champion Roofing was the first company in Oklahoma to install Brava synthetic roofing.

Can you repair a tile or slate roof rather than replace it?

Frequently, yes, and it is a large part of what we do on these roofs. The difficulty is usually matching: finding a product that matches the existing roof closely enough that the repair does not read as a patch from the street.

We maintain specialty roofs in Nichols Hills, Gaillardia, Rose Creek, Cobblestone and Heritage Hills, so this is routine work for us rather than an unusual request.

Is synthetic tile as good as the real thing?

For a lot of applications it is the better choice, which is why it has become so common on the higher end streets around the metro. Synthetic products are lighter than clay, concrete or natural slate, which matters on structures that were not designed for the load, and they are considerably less brittle.

They are not identical to natural material and some people prefer the real thing. That is a genuine preference rather than a technical argument, and we install both.

Were you really the first to install Brava in Oklahoma?

Yes. The owner met the product's founder, who came out to a church project intending to demonstrate the installation, and by the end of it our crew had worked out the method and were showing him.

It was a difficult install and the crew kept at it until they had it right. That is roughly how specialty work goes here.
